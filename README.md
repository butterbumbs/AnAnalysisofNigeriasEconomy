# An Analysis of Nigeria's Economy

## Overview

This report examines Nigeria's economic development across four lenses: GDP per capita, physical capital accumulation, human capital, and financial inclusion. Using data from the Maddison Project Database, the African Development Bank, the World Bank, and EFInA, the analysis argues that Nigeria's growth potential is constrained not by resource scarcity but by institutional failures in governance, education, and equitable policy.

## Key Findings

-   Nigeria's GDP per capita (PPP-adjusted) grew 322.5% from 1950–2024, but with severe downturns tied to political instability and oil dependency.
-   Gross fixed capital formation has recovered since 2018 and surpassed the world average share of GDP by 2023, though this has not translated proportionally into per capita GDP growth.
-   Nigeria's Human Capital Index (0.42) is well below the world average (0.65), driven by underfunded education and a 20.4% gender literacy gap.
-   Financial exclusion fell from 40% (2012) to 26% (2023) following the National Financial Inclusion Strategy, but rural residents and women remain disproportionately excluded.
